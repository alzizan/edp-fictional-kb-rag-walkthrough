# Solstice Commerce Group — Internal Data Glossary

Solstice Commerce Group is a fictional online retailer. The terms below are internal jargon used
only by its data and business teams; they do not exist in any public retail or e-commerce
literature.

## Velocity Tier

Velocity Tier classifies each customer by how OFTEN and how RECENTLY they buy. There are four
tiers: Surge (buys very often, last purchase very recent), Steady (stable buying pattern), Fading
(buying less often than before), and Dormant (no purchase for a long time). It is calculated per
customer from the dates of that customer's orders. The retention team uses Velocity Tier to
decide which customers to contact first.

## Glimmer Score

Glimmer Score measures how attractive a product is compared with other products in the same
category. It combines the product's price relative to its category and how often buyers end up
ordering that product instead of other products in the same category. A high score means the
product stands out in its category.

## Drift Margin

Drift Margin is the difference between a customer's typical order value (the average of their
past orders) and the value of their most recent order. A large Drift Margin, up or down, signals
an unusual change in buying behaviour: a successful upsell, a customer drifting away, or possible
fraud.

## Nova Segment

Nova Segment groups customers by WHEN they first signed up (their signup cohort) combined with
WHERE they are located (their country). The marketing team uses it to target regional campaigns at
a specific "generation" of customers.

## Orbit Cycle

Orbit Cycle is the average time between one order of a customer and their next order. A short
Orbit Cycle means the customer comes back often. An Orbit Cycle that keeps getting longer is an
early warning that the customer is about to move into the Fading Velocity Tier.

## Halo Effect Index

Halo Effect Index measures how likely a customer who just bought a product from one category is to
buy a product from a different, related category soon after. The team uses it for cross-selling:
which categories pull follow-up purchases in other categories.
